# Human Review and Control Plan: Macy's Marketing AI Coworker

## Overview

The AI coworker automates the data handoffs that M1 analysis found were "every single transfer is manual" across Macy's 6 to 8 week campaign cycle. But automation without oversight is worse than no automation at all: an AI that ships non-compliant copy to 30 million Star Rewards members creates more damage in minutes than a slow manual process would in weeks. Our M4 design preserves human authority at every step where business risk is highest while letting AI handle the pattern matching, scoring, and drafting that consumed most of the manual hours M1 documented. The runtime mechanisms that enforce this balance are the 5 Review actions (Approve, Edit, Reject, Rerun, Escalate), the Evidence screen that shows exactly what the AI used to produce its output, and the audit log that records every human decision with timestamps and persona identity.

## Persona Authority Map

| Persona | Role | Decision Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Merna (Campaign Manager) | Owns the campaign end-to-end | Creates briefs, selects segments, approves SKUs, owns Step 6 approval gate, confirms activation schedule |
| Abdullah (Senior Designer) | Creative lead | Reviews DAM asset shortlists, approves layout copy and visual direction at Steps 4-5 |
| Shankar (Marketing Analyst) | Data and insights | Reviews segmentation output, monitors live campaign KPIs, owns post-campaign reporting |
| Anna (Production Artist) | Localization and production | Spot-checks localization variants, approves variant sets before activation |
| Prof. Vincent (CEO) | Executive override | Receives all Escalate actions, can override any step's decision, reviews escalation queue |

Authority flows downward: any persona can Escalate to Prof. Vincent, but only the step owner can Approve or Reject at their gate. The CEO can intervene at any step but does not need to act on steps that clear review without escalation.

## What Humans See at Review Points

For the two most consequential review points — Step 6a (Compliance Pre Check) and Step 6b (Approval Brief Generator) — the following describes exactly what the human sees in our M4 UI.

### Step 6a: Compliance Pre Check Review Surface

The compliance card displays 3 findings (Brand Alignment, Disclaimers, Pricing Cross-Check), each with a color-coded status pill (green PASS, yellow WARN, red FAIL) and a one-sentence summary. Clicking "Show details" expands to the full AI reasoning with the cited document ID. A confidence indicator (High, Medium, or Low) appears next to the card header, derived from the number of failing checks: zero fails is High, one fail is Medium, two or more is Low.

The "Evidence" pill button on the card opens a side panel showing 5 collapsible sections: (1) the RAG documents retrieved, with document IDs, relevance ratings, and the actual text passages the AI used; (2) the `check_pricing_conflicts` MCP tool call showing the exact input SKU IDs and discount percentage alongside the tool's output JSON; (3) data sources consulted; (4) prior step outputs referenced; and (5) assumptions the system made. This is the surface where a reviewer catches Failure Cases 1, 2, and 3: if the cited passage does not match the AI's stated reasoning, or if the MCP tool output shows a suspicious PASS, the evidence makes it visible.

The Review screen at `/review?step=6a` presents the full AI output and the 5 action buttons: **Approve** (with confirmation modal), **Edit** (opens inline textareas preserving the original), **Reject** (requires a reason of at least 10 characters), **Rerun** (clears output and evidence, re-fires the skill), and **Escalate** (routes to CEO Vincent's queue with an optional reason). The live audit log at the bottom of the Review screen shows every prior action on this step with timestamps and persona identity.

### Step 6b: Approval Brief Generator Review Surface

The brief card displays 5 fields: Campaign Goal, Target Audience, Expected ROI, Risk Flags (as a bulleted list), and AI Recommendation (with a color-coded banner: green for "Approve," yellow for "Revise," red for "Reject"). A confidence indicator appears next to the header. If the brief was manually edited, an "(manually edited)" label appears, and a "Reset to AI version" link is available.

The "Evidence" pill opens a side panel showing which retrospective documents were retrieved (RETRO-Q4-2025, RETRO-SP-2025-BTY) for ROI benchmarking, and which prior step outputs were consumed (Step 6a compliance result). This is where a reviewer catches Failure Case 6: if the "Prior Step Outputs Referenced" section shows the Step 6a output was consumed but the brief's risk flags are empty or incoherent, that signals a cascade failure from a bad edit at 6a.

The "Sources used" panel expands to list all RAG documents cited, with document IDs. The Review screen at `/review?step=6b` provides the same 5 actions. Escalate routes to CEO Vincent, who sees the brief alongside its evidence trail and can make a final executive decision.

## Connection to M4 Testing

The human review points documented in this plan are the same gates where our DeepEval test suite (`tests/test_workflow.py`) exercises the AI outputs against adversarial and edge-case inputs. Automated tests verify that the AI produces correct, well-structured output under normal and boundary conditions; human review catches the failures that no automated test can anticipate — strategic misalignment, stale context, and the judgment calls that require business expertise. The combination of automated testing at every AI skill plus human review at the consequential gates (Steps 3, 5, 6a, 6b, 7, and 8) is what makes the AI coworker safe to deploy against Macy's nearly 30 million Star Rewards members.
